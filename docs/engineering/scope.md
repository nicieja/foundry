# Scope

The goal is the destination. A release is a step toward it. A release that does not reach the goal is correct — it is how an ambitious goal gets built at all.

`conventions.md` says how code is written. `evidence.md` says how a fuzzy goal is judged. This file says what is built now, and what waits.

It exists to prevent the failure that looks like rigor: scope taken from the goal, so the goal's whole ambition lands in the first release. Every market. Every platform. Every case. The loop digs until it has defined something nobody can build, and the digging is indistinguishable from work.

## The cut test

Three questions, in order. Anything that answers no to all three is a cut, not a task.

1. **Does the next verdict need it?** The next rung's bar, or a bullet under the current plan's Verification.
2. **Does the named behavior need it** — for this one person, at this one moment? Under a fuzzy goal that is the Behavior slot of the spec. Otherwise it is the milestone's "done when".
3. **Without it, is the artifact dishonest?** Money, safety, law, data loss, or a promise the artifact itself makes.

Question three is what keeps a cut honest. A thing you may not ship without is not scope; it is the floor.

## The cut is in breadth, never in craft

One market instead of every market. One platform. One segment. One arc. Ten of a thing where the goal implies a thousand.

What remains is held to the goal's own standard. A goal that says *not a cash grab* is not met by a smaller cash grab, and one that says *high quality* is not met by a narrow thing done badly. Cutting craft is not a cut. It is a different goal, and a worse one.

## Write the cut down

Two lines. Both, every time.

    docs/roadmap.md, under Later:
    - <work> — cut from <release> — revisit when: <the condition that brings it back>

    docs/decisions.md:
    - YYYY-MM-DD — <the cut> — <why it is not needed now> — revisit when: <trigger>

A cut with no trigger is a deletion wearing a kinder word. The trigger is a condition the loop can observe — a rung that passes, a rate that holds, a release that lands — never a date.

## Research runs to a budget

Every research dispatch carries its budget, written into the brief before it runs: the market or segment, how many comparables, how many sources, and the question that ends it.

The budget is registered the way a bar is. It is written before the material exists, and it does not move once material starts coming back. A dispatch with no budget has no stopping condition, and a researcher with no stopping condition returns when it runs out of context, not when the question is answered.

Breadth in research is cut by the same test as breadth in a release. *What the law says in every country we might ever enter* is not a research question. *What the law says in the one market this release serves* is.

## The first release under a fuzzy goal

R3's artifact is the first release: the thinnest thing a person can walk. R4 and R5 widen it.

The spec does not shrink with it. The Arithmetic needs the goal's full number and the Rate is the rate the goal requires, so both stay at goal size while the release does not. A spec cut down to what the current release can reach is how a goal quietly becomes whatever was easy to build.

## Tiers

Reducing a release's scope is T2: cut, write both lines, and flag the Done entry ` — review`.

Narrowing the Goal section is T3. The goal is the user's, and no amount of evidence about what is hard makes it yours.
